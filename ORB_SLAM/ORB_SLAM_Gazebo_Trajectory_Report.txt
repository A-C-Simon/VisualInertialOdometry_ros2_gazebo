ORB-SLAM ROS 2 Gazebo Trajectory Comparison Report
Date: 21 September 2026

1. Introduction

The objective was to test the Gazebo rover simulation with the ROS 2 ORB-SLAM visual odometry node, record the estimated visual trajectory together with the rover ground-truth trajectory, and determine whether differences between the two paths were caused by timing, coordinate frames, or visual tracking failures.

2. ROS 2 Gazebo integration

The Gazebo rover simulation was connected to the ROS 2 ORB-SLAM stereo node. The node consumed the simulated stereo camera topics and published the estimated camera pose, path, tracking state, tracked map points, and TF data. The simulation was configured for camera-only operation. The IMU and the previous OpenVINS runtime were not used.

The relevant recorded topics were:

  /orbslam3/pose
  /orbslam3/tracking_state
  /model_states
  /tf
  /tf_static
  /clock

The ORB-SLAM pose was published in the ORB-SLAM map frame. Gazebo model states provided the rover ground-truth pose, and TF provided the simulated rover frame relationships.

3. Initial recording and tracking failure

The first recording showed that ORB-SLAM could track the rover for a period, but later entered RECENTLY_LOST and LOST states. The terminal output reported repeated failures to track the local map, followed by relocalization attempts and creation of a new map.

When a new map was created, the ORB-SLAM pose returned to a new map coordinate system. The green path in RViz therefore resumed near its map origin while the Gazebo rover continued from its actual physical position. This made the complete green path unsuitable for direct comparison with the rover ground-truth path.

4. Recording of the valid trajectory interval

A second recording was made as orb_gazebo_run2. It contained the ORB-SLAM tracking-state topic and the Gazebo model-state topic required for direct comparison.

The first tracking loss occurred at approximately bag time 1790004260.229 seconds. The recording was trimmed immediately before that first RECENTLY_LOST transition. The valid portion was saved as:

  /home/ac/ORB_SLAM/orb_gazebo_run2_pre_reset

The trimmed recording contained approximately 46.6 seconds of data and ended while tracking was still OK. It contained 418 ORB-SLAM poses, 4,306 Gazebo model-state messages, 418 tracking-state messages, TF data, static TF data, and simulation clock data.

5. Timestamp comparison

Each ORB-SLAM pose was matched to the nearest Gazebo rover ground-truth pose using the recorded ROS 2 bag timestamps.

The timestamp results were:

  Mean absolute timestamp difference: 3.4 ms
  95th percentile timestamp difference: 6.2 ms
  Largest observed startup difference: approximately 216 ms

The normal operating timestamp difference was small compared with the camera frame interval. Timestamp synchronization was therefore not the primary cause of the green-path discrepancy. The startup outlier should still be handled by discarding or synchronizing the initial warm-up interval during future analysis.

6. Coordinate-frame comparison

The raw ORB-SLAM pose and Gazebo pose were not in the same coordinate frame. ORB-SLAM reported the camera pose in its map frame. Gazebo reported the rover body pose in the simulation ground-truth frame. The camera position and rover body position also differ by the fixed camera-to-rover mounting transform.

The first and last raw positions therefore did not overlay directly. The raw endpoint difference was approximately 4.03 m even though both trajectories described the same rover motion.

A rigid three-dimensional alignment was fitted using the synchronized trajectory samples. This alignment accounted for the different map orientation, map origin, and fixed camera-to-rover offset. After applying the fitted rigid transform, the trajectory comparison produced:

  RMS position error: 8.65 cm
  Median position error: 6.08 cm
  95th percentile position error: 15.4 cm
  Ground-truth path length: 6.35 m
  ORB-SLAM path length: 7.18 m

The aligned result showed that the retained ORB-SLAM trajectory followed the rover route reasonably well. The remaining difference was consistent with visual odometry drift and camera-to-body motion, rather than a timestamp failure.

7. Required coordinate-frame correction

The comparison identified the correction required for the ROS 2 simulation. The ORB-SLAM camera pose will be converted to the rover base pose using the calibrated camera-to-rover extrinsic transform. The map frame will then be initialized against the Gazebo odometry or world frame using the first synchronized valid ORB-SLAM and ground-truth poses.

The current fixed map-to-odom offset will be replaced or initialized from this measured relationship. This will allow the green RViz path to start at the rover's actual simulated position and remain in the same coordinate frame as the ground-truth path.

8. Results and conclusion

The Gazebo script was successfully tested with the ROS 2 ORB-SLAM visual odometry node. Both the green ORB-SLAM trajectory and the rover ground-truth trajectory were recorded in ROS 2 bags. The first complete recording demonstrated that map resets after tracking loss can restart the green path at a new origin. The valid pre-reset interval was isolated for a fair comparison.

The timestamp analysis showed that normal synchronization was within a few milliseconds. The large raw path difference was caused mainly by coordinate-frame and camera-to-rover alignment. After rigid alignment, the retained trajectory had an RMS difference of 8.65 cm and a 95th percentile difference of 15.4 cm. The next implementation step will apply this alignment directly in the ROS 2 node and launch configuration, then repeat the same bag-based comparison over a longer tracking interval.
