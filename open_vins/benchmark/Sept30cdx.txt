Work report for 29 and 30 September 2026
HW290 stereo VIO repair and computational cost

Objective

I worked on stopping false metre and kilometre OpenVINS trajectories from my
rigid HW290 stereo and IMU assembly. I also reduced sensor processing cost and
prepared comparable OpenVINS and ORB-SLAM3 diagnostics.

==================== 29 SEPTEMBER 2026 ====================

C++ IMU bridge

I implemented an event-driven C++ IMU bridge and made it the hardware default.
It retains checksums, source timestamps, SI conversion, exclusive serial access
and fault detection. A 15-second comparison delivered the same 1,400 samples:

                         Python bridge    C++ bridge
Accumulated CPU             4.355 s         0.188 s
Average of one core          25.51%           1.13%
Peak memory                60.27 MiB       21.63 MiB

The bridge CPU reduction was about 96%. Protocol parity and serial fault tests
covered 653 inputs, disconnects, saturation, clock reset and source-rate loss.
Artifact: benchmark/2026-09-29_CPP_IMU_Bridge.md.

Sensor failure handling

I traced early ORB resets and OpenVINS flights to IMU delivery as low as 14.3
Hz and, in one run, about 4 Hz. I added a full IMU reset, register verification,
100 Hz source counters, I2C fault reporting and separate host/source-rate checks.
Both launchers now wait for a verified 80 to 120 Hz stream. RViz remains
available when the IMU fails.

Git checkpoint

I committed and pushed separate camera timestamp, mount calibration, C++
bridge, firmware, launch supervision, ORB optimization and documentation work.

==================== 30 SEPTEMBER 2026 ====================

Bounded short tests

I excluded one incorrect recording. A repeated nominal 20 cm test produced
18.55 cm maximum displacement, 1.52 cm final residual and 1.76 mm initial
jitter. A 90-second rest check changed by 1.02 cm. A later free-motion run,
kept below about 30 cm horizontally and 20 cm vertically, reached 25.62 cm and
ended 6.25 cm from its initial estimate. These tests had no timed ground truth.
Artifacts: benchmark/results/hw290_20260930_motion_2/,
benchmark/results/hw290_20260930_rest_after_motion/ and
benchmark/results/hw290_20260930_continuous_motion_1/.

Camera and recording path

I enabled reliable image delivery in the ORB hardware wrapper. I converted the
OpenVINS splitter output from BGR8 to MONO8, reducing image payload by 67% with
identical grayscale pixels. I improved bag shutdown, resource summaries,
trajectory-bound warnings and interrupted-test reporting.

Desk-scale failure and I2C investigation

I used a 140 by 80 by 60 cm movement volume with a conservative 1.720 m
diagonal bound. Early desk runs reached 318.93 m. I found I2C read errors and
missing sequence numbers near some flight onsets, plus a separate serial
catch-up condition. I repaired the catch-up guard without weakening source
rate and gap checks.

I measured 4.65 V at VCC_IN, SDA and SCL and 3.32 V at the regulated rail while
I2C polling was stopped. Continuity passed. I reduced the I2C clock to 50 kHz,
shortened the moving leads from about 50 to 17 cm, soldered the Nano ends and
secured the module ends. A 25-second check averaged 99.15 Hz without I2C faults
or sequence loss.
Artifacts: benchmark/results/firmware_i2c_50khz_20260930/ and
benchmark/results/hw290_20260930_openvins_desk_3/.

Clean-IMU failure and visual-update loss

With the shortened wiring, a two-minute run had no I2C faults, sequence gaps or
saturation, but reached 1,524.24 m during capture and 23,846.83 m before stop.
The first full second without retained visual corrections began at 58.996
seconds; the path crossed the desk bound 1.9 seconds later.

A frame near the loss was blurred and overexposed: 56.1% of pixels exceeded
245, Laplacian variance was 14.9 and only 376 left-image ORB features remained.
Later images regained texture and stereo matches, but accepted visual updates
did not recover. Clean IMU communication alone therefore does not prevent the
runaway.
Artifacts: benchmark/results/hw290_20260930_openvins_desk_4/ and
benchmark/results/hw290_openvins_20260930_164302_BK9ali/.

Tracker and innovation-gate experiments

Descriptor replay exposed an OpenVINS crash on empty or incompatible descriptor
matrices. I added a C++ guard and rebuilt. Descriptor mode still could not
initialize reliably on this bag, so I kept KLT for hardware operation.

Increasing the MSCKF and SLAM innovation gates from 1x to 5x made the original
failed bag replay stay within 0.958 m and end at 0.336 m. It retained visual
updates on 8,931 of 8,985 frames; the longest complete dropout was 0.60 seconds.
Artifact: benchmark/results/hw290_openvins_20260930_164302_BK9ali/klt_chi5_replay/.

Physical validation after replay

The 5x setting did not generalize. A physical run stayed bounded for about 132
seconds, then one ±4 g or ±500 degree/s sample saturated. The estimate crossed
1.720 m 2.33 seconds later, reached 413.51 m and ended 359.49 m from its start.
The bus had no I2C errors or sequence gaps.
Artifact: benchmark/results/hw290_20260930_openvins_desk_chi5_1/.

I added a versioned IMU3 format, widened the range to ±8 g and ±1000 degree/s,
retained the 50 kHz bus and updated both decoders. I changed the camera from
automatic 15.6 ms exposure to fixed 5 ms exposure. The still scene retained
about 1,400 ORB features per lens with fewer clipped highlights.

The final recorded motion test had zero I2C errors, sequence gaps and saturation,
but still reached 39.90 m by 84 seconds. I stopped it and saved the diagnostic
bag. Exposure, range, wiring and the 5x gate do not solve the estimator loss.
Artifacts: benchmark/results/hw290_20260930_openvins_desk_imu3_manual1/ and
benchmark/results/hw290_openvins_20260930_173808_NIXPhn/.

Computational cost

The clean-IMU diagnostic run averaged 127.3% of one CPU core overall. OpenVINS
used 50.9% and 163.3 MiB; 12,739 updates averaged 13.26 ms. The latest full
diagnostic run averaged 137.2% overall; OpenVINS used 62.2% and 143.2 MiB, with
12.48 ms mean update time. Recording and RViz are included, so these are not
final OpenVINS versus ORB-SLAM3 benchmark figures.

Current conclusion

I removed the observed wiring, bus-rate, saturation and exposure explanations,
but OpenVINS still loses visual correction during continued motion and then
integrates the IMU into a false trajectory. The final bag preserves the exact
loss for replay. Long-motion stability and the final OpenVINS versus ORB-SLAM3
accuracy comparison remain incomplete.
