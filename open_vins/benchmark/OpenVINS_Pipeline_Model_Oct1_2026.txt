1. HIGH LEVEL OVERVIEW
======================

OpenVINS estimates the position, orientation and velocity of my IMU, together
with sensor biases. The IMU predicts motion between camera frames. Features
tracked in the stereo images correct that prediction. Calibration connects
the two measurements in space, time and units. RViz displays the resulting
estimate; it does not calculate or correct the trajectory.

NORMAL PIPELINE

  Stereo camera                           HW290 accelerometer + gyroscope
  1280x480 image, about 30 Hz              About 100 measurements/second
           |                                           |
  usb_cam: capture timestamp               Nano: I2C read + MCU timestamp
           |                                           |
  C++ stereo_splitter                      C++ hw290_imu serial bridge
  split, grayscale, rectify                validate packets, scale units,
           |                               map MCU time to host time
  /cam0/image_raw + /cam1/image_raw                      |
           |                                         /imu0
           |                                           |
           +------ time alignment + initialization ----+
                                  |
                     IMU prediction / propagation
                                  |
                 Stereo KLT feature tracking and geometry
                                  |
                 Visual residual checks and EKF correction
                    ^             |
                    |             v
                    +--- corrected state and covariance
                                  |
                    pose + path + odometry + TF
                                  |
                                 RViz

                 Calibration feeds the prediction,
                 camera geometry and visual correction.

OBSERVED FAILURE CHAIN

  Useful visual constraints are lost or rejected
                         |
  Too few accepted MSCKF / local landmark corrections
                         |
  IMU prediction continues with insufficient correction
                         |
  Orientation, bias or acceleration error accumulates
                         |
  False velocity and position grow, sometimes extremely far
                         |
  RViz draws the false estimate outside the physical workspace

The observed failure point was loss of useful visual corrections around the
visual-update stage. Replacing the camera geometry, camera/IMU transform and
time offset together removed the kilometer-scale flight in replay and passed
a fresh physical desk test. This supports calibration mismatch as a contributor;
it does not isolate which individual parameter initiated the failure.
Image quality and input delivery can also interrupt the visual constraints.

My bounded desk test allows a 1.40 x 0.80 x 0.60 m workspace. Its maximum
corner-to-corner distance is about 1.72 m. One recorded trajectory crossed
that bound after about 64.1 seconds and reached 7.46 m. This establishes a
failure even though I did not measure the exact path at every instant.

With the new calibration, the same recording stayed below 0.47 m displacement
instead of reaching 18.92 km. The fresh physical run stayed below 0.852 m across
868 seconds of poses, including a two-minute continuous desk-motion test.
I stayed within the limits and saw no flights. No trajectory bounds or origin
resets were introduced. See section 2.10 for the checks and remaining limits.


2. COMPREHENSIVE MODEL
======================

2.1 Launch and operating modes
------------------------------

Normal launch:
  ./hw290_stereo/run_hw290_openvins.sh

The launcher starts camera acquisition, the stereo splitter, the IMU bridge,
static camera transforms, OpenVINS and RViz. The normal ROS domain is 48 unless
the environment overrides it. All participating ROS processes must use the
same domain. Startup waits for verified IMU delivery around 80-120 Hz before
starting the estimator.

If the IMU fails startup, RViz starts in sensor view, but no valid VIO estimate
is available. Reconnecting USB does not itself prove that the I2C sensor is
working. The verified IMU stream must be restored and the launcher restarted.

Relevant optional modes:
  --diagnostics       Record sensor/pose topics and detailed state/update logs.
  --sensors-only      Run sensors without estimating a trajectory.
  --no-rviz           Remove visualization cost.
  --raw-stereo        Unrectified images; requires sensors-only mode.
  --calibration-screen  Raw sensor capture with the fullscreen AprilGrid GUI.
  --imu-allan         Stationary IMU recording for noise characterization.

The measurement-heavy parts are already compiled C++: camera driver, stereo
splitter, IMU bridge, OpenVINS and calibration GUI. Shell and Python helpers
manage processes, configuration and reports. Their presence alone does not
explain estimator cost or trajectory divergence.

2.2 Camera acquisition and stereo preprocessing
-----------------------------------------------

The USB camera delivers a 1280x480 side-by-side frame at a nominal 30 Hz. The
usb_cam node decodes its MJPEG stream. The local driver fixes timestamp clock
conversion, preserving capture timing instead of substituting callback arrival
time. The splitter's automatic arrival-time correction is disabled.

The current launcher sets and reads back manual 5 ms exposure, gain 255,
brightness 0 and backlight compensation 0. These settings restored texture in
the tested desk lighting. They are not a universal lighting calibration.

The native splitter makes two 640x480 monochrome images and normally rectifies
them with the stereo camera calibration. It publishes:
  /cam0/image_raw       Left image
  /cam1/image_raw       Right image
  /cam0/camera_info     Left camera model
  /cam1/camera_info     Right camera model

Despite their topic names, these images are RECTIFIED during normal VIO.
Their model in OpenVINS therefore uses the rectified focal lengths and zero
distortion. A raw image must not be paired with that rectified model. OpenVINS
loads its geometry from YAML; publishing CameraInfo alone does not update it.

Two matching image timestamps allow the stereo callback to form one stereo
measurement. Splitting one combined frame provides matching source timestamps;
this does not independently verify exposure synchronization inside the camera.

Failure checks here: darkness, clipping, blur, decoding/frame loss, inconsistent
rectification, inaccurate stereo baseline, and wrong capture timestamps.

2.3 IMU acquisition, transport and units
---------------------------------------

HW290 -> Nano I2C -> timestamped serial packets -> hw290_imu -> /imu0.

Firmware identifies the chip, configures approximately 100 Hz output, and
reads raw acceleration, angular rate and temperature. The recorded identity
0x98 is compatible with the ICM20689 path; the firmware also supports a 0x68
device. Configured measurement ranges are +/-8 g and +/-1000 degrees/second.

IMU3 packets contain sample sequence, acquisition micros, raw values,
temperature and a checksum. The native host bridge validates packets and
tracks resets, gaps, corrupt packets, source/delivery rate and saturation.
It maps MCU acquisition time into the ROS host clock rather than assigning
the same receipt timestamp to every sample in a serial burst.

Published units:
  acceleration: m/s^2, including the stationary gravity-related measurement
  angular rate: rad/s
  frame: imu
  orientation: unavailable, marked by orientation_covariance[0] = -1

No onboard attitude or DMP trajectory is fused. OpenVINS estimates orientation
and gravity alignment from the camera and raw inertial measurements.

Failure checks here: I2C read faults, power/contact problems, wrong sensor
range/scaling, axis conventions, timing/reset errors, saturation and biases.
Clean serial delivery verifies transport, not complete physical calibration.

2.4 Calibration joins the camera and IMU models
----------------------------------------------

The active configuration files are:
  hw290_stereo/estimator_config.yaml
  hw290_stereo/calibration/20261001/camchain.yaml
  hw290_stereo/calibration/20261001/imu.yaml

The separate stereo rectification file is:
  hw290_stereo/calibration/20261001/stereo_opencv.yaml

Current rectified camera intrinsics, both cameras:
  fx = fy = 367.53325092 pixels
  cx = 324.01826477, cy = 268.30197144 pixels
  resolution = 640x480; pinhole/radtan with zero remaining distortion
  stereo baseline approximately 0.05945 m

Spatial calibration:
  T_imu_cam maps camera coordinates into IMU coordinates.
  p_IMU = R_IMU_CAM * p_CAM + t_IMU_CAM

The loader also accepts the inverse convention T_cam_imu and inverts it when
needed. Rotation, translation and transform direction must all agree. The
current mount transform comes from the October 1 Kalibr fit, rotated into each
rectified camera frame. Its rotation differs from the previous transform by
about 26.5 degrees. The fitted 6.7 cm lever arm still needs a better physical
check against the rough 4 cm measurement. Provisional IMU noise was used in
the fit. The static camera TF uses this same selected calibration chain.

Temporal calibration:
  t_IMU = t_camera + timeshift_cam_imu
  current fixed offset = +0.013040460 seconds

The image queue waits until the IMU buffer covers this shifted camera time.
This compensates a relative offset; it is not hardware synchronization. The
loader uses cam0's offset for the stereo system. Wrong offset during rotation
means the visual observation and predicted pose refer to different instants.

IMU noise parameters currently loaded:
  accelerometer noise density  0.02
  accelerometer random walk    0.003
  gyroscope noise density      0.005
  gyroscope random walk        0.00002

These are provisional inputs, not results from a completed Allan measurement.
They control predicted uncertainty and how residuals are weighted. IMU scale
and axis calibration matrices are identity; gyro gravity sensitivity is zero.

Online estimation of camera intrinsics, extrinsics, time offset, IMU intrinsics
and gyro gravity sensitivity is currently disabled. Bias estimation remains
active. A configuration comment describing a previous bounded replay does not
establish that the same setting solves new physical runs.

Official guidance warns that spatial and temporal calibration errors can
quickly degrade dynamic trajectories. This supports measuring those values;
it does not establish the cause of my particular failure.
Source: https://docs.openvins.com/gs-calibration.html

2.5 Initialization and the local reference frame
------------------------------------------------

The current static initializer uses a 2-second measurement window, IMU
threshold 0.5 and image-disparity threshold 2 pixels. Dynamic initialization
is disabled. It needs sufficiently stationary, usable measurements to recover
an initial gravity direction, biases and state.

The resulting global frame is a local reference anchored by initialization.
It is not a surveyed Earth coordinate system. Absolute position and yaw have
no external reference in this setup. Metric scale relies on consistent stereo
geometry and inertial measurements.

Zero-velocity updates are enabled only at the beginning, using the IMU-based
test. They assist startup; they are not a continuous desk-motion bound.

A missing global TF before initialization can produce RViz's fixed-frame error.
The actual normal RViz fixed frame is 'global'. The dynamic global -> imu TF
is published after initialization, with a one-second guard in its publishing
path, and requires successful propagation. Shaking is not a calibration step
or a reliable remedy for missing initialization.

2.6 State and IMU prediction
----------------------------

The core inertial state contains:
  q: orientation; p: position; v: velocity
  bg: gyroscope bias; ba: accelerometer bias

The EKF also carries uncertainty/cross-correlation, recent pose clones and
selected local landmarks. Up to 11 pose clones and 50 local SLAM landmarks
are configured. Camera calibration values are held fixed in this setup.

Conceptual prediction, using R from IMU to global coordinates:
  corrected angular rate = measured angular rate - bg
  global acceleration   = R * (measured specific force - ba) + gravity
  dv/dt = global acceleration
  dp/dt = velocity

The implementation uses RK4 mean integration and propagates uncertainty using
the IMU noise model. Biases are modeled as slowly varying states. Stationary
accelerometer output is not zero; correct orientation and gravity handling
make the estimated translational acceleration approximately zero.

Why loss of correction can grow so severely:
  constant acceleration error a produces position error roughly 0.5*a*t^2.
  A 0.01 m/s^2 uncorrected error would give about 18 m after 60 seconds.
  A 1-degree tilt error can leak about 0.17 m/s^2 of gravity into translation.

These are illustrative integration effects, not measured error values for my
rig. Incorrect geometry, bias or timing can also make later visual residuals
larger, reinforcing rejection and divergence.

2.7 Visual tracking, triangulation and correction
-------------------------------------------------

KLT optical flow tracks image features across frames and the stereo pair.
Current front-end settings include num_pts=250, FAST threshold 10, an 8x6
grid, 10-pixel minimum spacing, histogram equalization and no downsampling.
Tracking is capped at 35 Hz, above the nominal 30 Hz camera input. OpenCV is
configured for four threads. The ordinary VIO ArUco tracker is disabled.

Recent poses are cloned so a track can constrain motion over multiple frames.
Lost tracks, tracks touching a retiring clone and long tracks supply candidate
MSCKF constraints. The configured maximum is 40 candidates per MSCKF update.

For each candidate, the updater:
  1. Cleans observations and checks usable track length.
  2. Triangulates/refines its 3D position and checks geometry.
  3. Computes predicted pixels, residuals and Jacobians.
  4. Removes the feature-position nuisance variables by nullspace projection.
  5. Checks residual consistency against predicted uncertainty.
  6. Combines surviving constraints and applies an EKF correction.

Innovation check, in simplified notation:
  residual r = observed measurement - predicted measurement
  S = H*P*H^T + measurement noise R
  consistency statistic = r^T * S^-1 * r

The current MSCKF and local landmark gates use a multiplier of 5 on the
95-percent chi-square threshold, with 1-pixel measurement noise. Rejected
tracks are removed from the update. If no constraints survive, that visual
update cannot correct the inertial state. Loosening the gate can admit bad
measurements and cannot substitute for accurate calibration.

Local SLAM landmarks provide additional EKF corrections. Up to 25 existing
landmarks are handled per sequential update batch; new landmarks use delayed
initialization, with a configured startup delay of 2 seconds. Old clones and
unusable landmarks are marginalized to bound filter size.

This local filter does not provide ORB-SLAM3's global map optimization or loop
closure recovery. Revisiting the desk is not guaranteed to remove accumulated
drift. A high image-feature count does not prove that this updater accepted
valid geometric constraints.

2.8 Output, TF, recording and computational cost
------------------------------------------------

Main outputs in the launcher's /ov_msckf namespace:
  /ov_msckf/poseimu    Estimated IMU pose with covariance
  /ov_msckf/pathimu    Accumulated estimated IMU trajectory
  /ov_msckf/odomimu    Propagated pose/velocity output
  global -> imu       Dynamic pose transform
  imu -> cam0/cam1    Static transforms from the configured mount model

The IMU callback can publish propagated odometry between image updates. The
camera update path publishes the filter estimate and path. A continuing TF or
path therefore does not prove that visual corrections are still working.

The run summary measures child-process accumulated user+system CPU time and
peak process RSS. 100% CPU means one fully occupied core. Per-process peak
memory values are not a simultaneous total. Diagnostic recording and viewers
add costs and must be matched when comparing pipelines.

Example measured run: hw290_openvins_20261001_103252_39j3QM
  Process       Average CPU, one-core basis    Peak RSS
  Estimator              63.5%                141.9 MiB
  Stereo splitter        33.6%                 50.1 MiB
  Camera                 13.5%                 97.6 MiB
  IMU bridge              1.4%                 23.4 MiB
  RViz                   21.9%                184.4 MiB

Measured child CPU total: 348.62 seconds over 266.62 seconds launch wall time,
or 130.8% of one core. Each process has its own lifetime denominator. This run
had diagnostics disabled. Its 7,701 logged camera processing intervals averaged
11.74 ms, with a 19.1 ms 95th percentile. These processing intervals are not
the sensor frame period or a complete end-to-end latency measurement.

This is a measured OpenVINS example, not a matched ORB-SLAM3 comparison or
proof of good accuracy. A faster implementation can still estimate bad poses.

2.9 Failure evidence and remaining uncertainty
----------------------------------------------

Confirmed findings from my recordings:

  A. An earlier failure included dark images and collapse of useful visual
     updates. Fixed exposure restored visible texture, but later motion tests
     still diverged. Exposure was therefore not a complete fix.

  B. The bounded desk run crossed 1.72 m at 64.10 seconds and reached 7.4649 m.
     The sampled segment contains 2,612 poses over 87.01 seconds. IMU delivery
     stayed around 99 Hz with no reported packet, I2C or saturation faults in
     the associated reviewed run. Those transport checks did not prevent drift.

  C. About 1,480 offline ORB image features were visible in the desk scene.
     This is an image diagnostic, not the OpenVINS KLT count and not an accepted
     MSCKF-update count. It cannot prove healthy visual correction.

  D. A large acceleration spike occurred around 83.4 seconds, after the first
     workspace escape around 64.1 seconds. It does not explain that initial
     escape by timing alone.

  E. A separate run recorded 44.75 m displacement with clean IMU delivery.
     Its physical movement was not measured, so it is not an accuracy score.

  F. A replay with online spatial/time calibration estimated approximately
     8.4-10.5 ms offset but still diverged to approximately 1,577 m. That offset
     did not validate that replay's replacement for the previous 20 ms setting.

Detailed failed-run logs identify visual constraint rejection/loss followed by
continued inertial propagation. The bounded desk recording establishes the
trajectory failure; its default INFO logs do not identify the exact rejected
track or calibration parameter responsible at the 64-second crossing.

Open questions and the measurements needed to resolve them:
  Camera/IMU mount transform   Kalibr rotation/translation and residual report
  Relative timing             Acquisition stamps, estimated offset, held-out run
  IMU noise and bias behavior  Long stationary data and Allan analysis
  Camera/stereo geometry      Raw-image calibration and reprojection errors
  Feature failure trigger     Accepted-update counts, rejection logs, image replay

The fitted camera geometry, spatial transform and timing have now passed
the tests below. Exact mount translation and measured IMU noise remain open.

Separate fault branches:
  No USB enumeration: device/connection problem before ROS acquisition.
  WHO_AM_I=0/read_ok=0: sensor I2C startup failure, not successful IMU delivery.
  No global frame/no pose: initialization, stream or TF publishing problem.
  Pose exists but escapes the desk: estimator accuracy/divergence problem.

I shortened and secured the wiring. Clean recent transport counters make an
active communication fault less supported for those reviewed failures, but
do not certify all future runs or eliminate unmeasured sensor/model errors.

2.10 Calibration branch and validation plan
-------------------------------------------

The screen-calibration mode follows a separate branch:
  Raw stereo + IMU -> native AprilGrid detection/readiness -> recorded bag
  -> ROS 2 to ROS 1 bag conversion -> Kalibr camera and camera/IMU calibration
  -> inspect residuals and transforms -> consistent VIO configuration
  -> held-out replay -> physical continuous-motion desk validation.

The target is a 6x6 AprilGrid with approximately 40 mm tag edges and 12 mm
gaps; tagSpacing=0.3. Its displayed physical size must remain fixed. A screen
target's ruler measurement, display scaling and flatness limit metric accuracy.
I checked the fullscreen scale separately after confirming that the earlier
measurement came from a browser. The saved fullscreen measurement also gives
40 mm edges, with a 1440-pixel target on a 2560x1440 display.
The GUI now uses Kalibr's native detector because the earlier preview detector
rejected images that Kalibr accepted. That was a capture-readiness fault; it
was not the ordinary OpenVINS KLT tracker.

The GUI checks fresh stereo frames, matched observations and 80-120 Hz IMU,
then manages recording and the red/green movement cues automatically. Green
means capture readiness, not validated calibration or a drift-free estimator.
The five-second countdown now tolerates brief detection/callback fluctuations.
It is not a vibration measurement. Sustained target loss or invalid IMU rate
restarts readiness; missing sensor streams end the capture as incomplete.
The recorder now saves and applies a reliable 1,000-sample IMU queue to absorb
brief stalls. A measurement-only window saves the fullscreen ruler measurement
without recording or requiring connected sensors.

A physical bag was saved today in screen_calibration_20261001_133319_434.
Its metadata contains 19,134 IMU messages and about 5,759 frames per camera
over 193.94 seconds. Those counts support nominal delivery; calibration
coverage, timestamp quality and fitted results still require inspection.

Later capture and fit results:
  screen_calibration_20261001_134827_547 completed about 95 seconds with 2,854
  frames per camera and 9,356 IMU samples. Its saved IMU stream has six gaps
  above 25 ms, including 309 ms. The larger recording queue passed a simulated
  load check at 100 Hz. The subsequent physical recording verification is below.
  Stereo reprojection component spreads were about 0.26-0.32 pixels.
  A diagnostic camera/IMU fit on the first 60 seconds, before the long gaps,
  estimated a cam0 offset of 13.04 ms and a rotation approximately 26.5 degrees
  from the previous transform after accounting for rectification. Its estimated
  6.7 cm lever arm exceeds the approximate 4 cm mount measurement. Noise values
  remain provisional. After replay and physical checks, this fit became the
  normal OpenVINS profile in calibration/20261001/.

Replay checks:
  Both old and fitted models used the same dynamic initialization settings and
  half-speed playback. Static initialization produced no trajectory because the
  capture begins in motion. The old model reached 18,919.61 m displacement;
  the fitted model stayed below 0.46848 m and retained visual features in 2,725
  of 2,741 updates. The baseline was rectified offline from raw mono images;
  rounding can differ slightly from rectifying RGB before conversion.

  An AprilGrid PnP reference shares camera observations and the fitted model,
  so it is not independent ground truth. A rigid alignment without scale fitting
  used the first 60 seconds. Beyond 60 seconds, position RMSE was 2.67 cm and
  maximum error was 3.54 cm across 68 samples. This checks transfer beyond the
  camera/IMU fit interval, with the shared-camera limitation.

Physical check:
  hw290_openvins_20261001_165958_VkGYYe used stationary initialization and the
  same mount. I completed about two minutes of continuous desk movement within
  the stated workspace and confirmed no flights. Across 18,718 poses spanning
  868.00 seconds, maximum displacement was 0.85166 m. The final 30-second
  per-axis position ranges were 2.77, 7.04 and 3.69 mm. Later waiting and possible
  repositioning mean final displacement cannot be treated as return error.

  The diagnostic run logged mean 14.82 ms and p95 23.70 ms tracking/update time.
  Estimator CPU averaged 56.46% of one core with 185.14 MiB peak RSS. Total
  measured child CPU averaged 125.59% of one core, including RViz and recording.

  The bag saved 92,748 IMU measurements at 99.02 Hz with a 22.02 ms maximum
  interval and no interval above 25 ms. OpenVINS still logged 46 input gaps
  above 50 ms, maximum 280.11 ms. This exposes an estimator delivery issue
  even though the recorded stream is complete. Counts also include different
  startup/shutdown times and must not be subtracted as a simple loss total.

Raw Kalibr results must be applied consistently: normal VIO uses rectified
images, so camera/IMU transforms must also account for rectification rotations,
and the YAML camera intrinsics must match the images actually supplied.

For IMU noise, the prepared Allan workflow requires a long stationary recording;
the short smoke test only verified the tool. OpenVINS recommends much longer
stationary collections, up to around 20 hours. Calibration checks also include
camera reprojection error, inertial residuals and sensible physical transforms.
Source: https://docs.openvins.com/gs-calibration.html

Kalibr's documented ROS 2 workflow converts the recording into a ROS 1 bag for
its calibration tools. My conversion tool and Kalibr build have been checked.
Source: https://github.com/ethz-asl/kalibr/wiki/ROS2-Calibration-Using-Kalibr

The checked replay and physical motion test cover the earlier delayed failure
duration and showed no gross divergence with the new profile. Staying inside the desk
bound rules out that gross failure, but centimeter accuracy still requires
measured motion or an independent ground-truth trajectory. Clipping the path,
resetting to the origin or stopping prediction would not validate the estimate.
